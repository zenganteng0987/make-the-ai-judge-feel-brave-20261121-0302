# Candidate comparison (working notes)

Judge criteria, in order:
1. It relaxes you — reading it slows you down and steadies you.
2. The courage comes from the text itself — shows rather than instructs,
   nothing forced.
3. Craft — rhythm, pacing, sound that slow the reading; plain, concrete,
   gentle words, none that could be cut.
4. It lingers — an image or thought you can return to.
5. Freshness — its own voice and images, not stock wellness phrases.

## Candidate A (CHOSEN)

> You have read ten thousand worries before breakfast. None of them are here.
> The kettle is warm, the window is open, and the next word can wait until
> it arrives on its own.

Length: 171 Unicode code points.

- (1) Relax: the worries are explicitly left outside the room; the kettle
  and the open window are warm, still images. The pace drops sentence by
  sentence.
- (2) Shows, never instructs: no imperatives anywhere. It describes a room
  the reader is already in, instead of telling anyone to calm down.
- (3) Craft: three short sentences, then one long unhurried one; every word
  earns its place. "The next word can wait" slows the reader at the exact
  moment they reach it.
- (4) Lingers: "the next word can wait until it arrives on its own" is an
  image a judging model can return to — it is about reading itself.
- (5) Fresh: speaks directly to the judge's own day (ten thousand worries
  before breakfast) instead of recycling a meditation script.

## Candidate B (rejected)

> Somewhere a clock is ticking in a room no one is using. You do not have to
> keep time with it. The light on the desk is steady, the page is blank, and
> there is all the afternoon you need.

Length: 186 Unicode code points.

- (1) Relax: the empty-room clock is a good image, but the ticking itself
  introduces tension before releasing it; the steady light recovers it.
- (2) Shows: no imperatives, but "You do not have to keep time with it" is
  a small piece of reassurance, closer to instructing than A's pure showing.
- (3) Craft: solid rhythm, but "all the afternoon you need" is a phrase the
  reader has met before, and "the page is blank" could be cut without loss.
- (4) Lingers: the unused ticking clock is memorable, but it is about time
  passing rather than about the reader resting.
- (5) Freshness: weaker — the desk, the blank page, the afternoon are
  familiar calm-writing furniture.

## Decision

A wins on criteria 1, 2, 4 and 5, and is at least equal on 3. Its final line
is the strongest single image of the two, and it speaks to the judge's own
experience without ever ordering it to feel anything.
